# v1.15 numerical protocol — hosted-runner candidate gates

**Status:** proposed for must-pin review, 2026-09-27. Implements the accepted
[scope](v1.15-scope.md) at `b22718f`. No calibration implementation, candidate
selection or fresh timing measurement is authorized by this draft alone.
Historical-log preservation and source inspection do not start confirmation.

## 1. Question and decisions to pin

**New prerequisite found during review:** the September 17 environment
capture reports Rust 1.98.1, although the setup action installed 1.95.0.
The repository's `rust-toolchain.toml` selects `stable`; both dudect workflows
invoke bare cargo. Installing a version did not establish which compiler
executed the bench. The historical fixed-1.95 assumption is therefore false.

This protocol explicitly proposes enforcing the **existing intended 1.95.0
pin** in both dudect jobs with job-level `RUSTUP_TOOLCHAIN=1.95.0`, plus
assertions of `rustc -Vv`, cargo identity and `rustup show active-toolchain`
before cache/build/measurement. The workflows' reviewed fingerprints and
policy checks must cover this effective selection. This changes actual
execution from a floating toolchain to the intended one; treat it as a
reviewed re-baseline, not a behavior-neutral documentation correction.
No threshold changes accompany the repair. A real red under the enforced
compiler still blocks a merge until investigated.

**Scope amendment to approve with this protocol:** enforce the existing pin
and collect a fresh 42-day calibration cohort before the 42-day confirmation
window. The older corpus remains preserved evidence, stratified by actual
recorded compiler; it cannot be relabeled as a Rust 1.95 baseline. No new
compiler version, runner label, production timeout or threshold is proposed.


Can a frozen, target/CPU/configuration-specific nightly bound demonstrate
both stable prospective behavior and detection of the control effects below,
without weakening the existing gate policy? The candidate table covers all
four demoted targets and records the existing fanout policy unchanged.

This protocol proposes **no relaxation above an existing bound and no
removal of a sentinel**. The September 17 breach remains unexplained and is
not evidence supporting either action. Instead, the rule can tighten a
qualified cell to a lower static bound. A cell unable to earn a change keeps
its current bound and its existing assurance limitations. This is a policy
choice to review, not an empirical finding that relaxation is impossible.

| Pin | Proposed value |
|---|---|
| Historical cutoff | `2026-09-27T12:00:00Z`, starting `2026-08-11T00:00:00Z` |
| Sampling unit | One scheduled first-attempt job per feature leg per UTC date |
| Cell | Exact target, normalized CPU model and exact feature leg |
| Fresh calibration cohort | Exactly 42 UTC dates after the effective pin and evidence capture are ready |
| Calibration minimum | 20 distinct UTC dates; at least 5 in each fixed 21-day half |
| Candidate formula | Twice the nearest-rank 90th percentile, rounded upward to 0.05, floor 0.20, never above the current bound |
| Prospective duration | Exactly 42 consecutive UTC dates, no early acceptance or extension |
| Prospective minimum | 20 distinct dates for every changed cell |
| Ordinary-run acceptance | Zero candidate-bound breaches in any eligible observation of a changed cell, throughout the entire window |
| Control coverage | At least 3 valid jobs on distinct dates per changed target/CPU/feature cell, both class directions, modest and gross effects |
| Added control budget | At most 12 four-leg dispatches (48 jobs), 90-minute timeout per job, at most 72 runner-hours, no automatic reruns |
| Activation set | All changed cells frozen together; no selection of successful cells afterward |
| Fallback | Existing policy for sparse, unknown, incompatible or unvalidated cells |

These are empirical pilot acceptance criteria, **not** a claimed bound on the
true false-alarm or leak-detection rate. With zero events in 20 independent,
identically distributed trials, the one-sided 95% binomial upper bound is
about 13.91%; even 42 such trials only gives about 6.89%. Hosted jobs may not
meet independence/stationarity assumptions. Report those limits instead of
calling a short green sequence proof of rare alarms. See the
[NIST exact-binomial construction](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm).

## 2. Evidence corpus and provenance

There are two separate corpora: the archived August–September observations
(descriptive evidence and investigation), and the new fixed-toolchain
calibration cohort (the sole input to candidate derivation). Preserve both
with the same schema. Never pool their compiler identities.

C0 is 00:00 UTC on the first date strictly after the reviewed pin-enforcement
PR is merged and immutable source/build manifests and evidence capture are
ready. Record C0 and C0+42 days before the first included scheduled job.
The calibration cohort spans `[C0, C0+42 days)`, with fixed halves of 21 days.
No early stopping, extension or replacement of unsuccessful draws.


The [archive manifest](v1.15-archive-manifest.csv) records a completed
read-only preservation pass: 72 first-attempt workflow archives, 7,699,741
bytes, all checksum-verified and ZIP-integrity-checked. Their metadata counts
48 scheduled and 24 manual runs; 66 succeeded, 5 failed and 1 was cancelled.
This is the full retrieval inventory, not the smaller twin-era eligible
corpus. Raw archives are retained locally. The independently reviewed
[historical extraction](https://github.com/frankxue831/gm-crypto-rs/blob/1d9ea61fd044c8e2c9e51807f4a4a9886f923a62/docs/v1.15-historical-evidence.md)
preserves 288 jobs and 26,471 finite per-pass results, with explicit absences
and exact reproduction of 4,675 printed gate-input lists. All 288 actual
environment captures report Rust 1.97.1, 1.98.0 or 1.98.1, never 1.95.0.
No missing archive was silently discarded.

Preserve every workflow run in the interval, including failed, cancelled,
manual and rerun attempts. The first draft's 63-run/252-job census is a
cross-check, not a filter that forces the extracted corpus to match it.
Preserve job IDs, attempt, timestamps, event, commit, workflow identity,
feature leg, CPU string, image/version, kernel, rustc/cargo versions,
per-pass bench outputs and seeds, and control/completeness verdicts. Keep
SHA-256 digests of source logs and extraction version. Record missing fields
explicitly. Do not infer an image version from its date or a CPU from tau.

Publish a compact per-pass CSV plus a per-job manifest and exclusion table.
Archive relevant source excerpts with public Actions URLs before retention
expires; exclude tokens, host paths and unrelated setup output from published
artifacts. Raw downloads stay local research material. Preserve the printed
signed `max t`, signed `max tau` and rounded crop sample count when present;
never reconstruct missing signs from the absolute gate summary.

The observation used for deriving bounds is the median of the five absolute
**five-decimal bench-reported** tau values. Four-decimal `runs=[...]` gate
summaries are cross-checks only; use neither their rounded numbers nor a new
normalization to silently change the gated observable. A five-decimal value
is parsed as a decimal; the gate's strict `median > bound` failure convention
is preserved, including equality passing. Nonfinite or missing values are
invalid, not zero. A job needs exactly one output for every required target
in each of the five numbered passes, with negative-control magnitude >1.0
in every pass. Invalid jobs remain in the manifest but cannot qualify a cell.

**Selection:** only `event=schedule`, `run_attempt=1`, in the fresh calibration
interval, with five complete passes at `DUDECT_SAMPLES=100000`, may derive
bounds. The historical cutoff only bounds the archived descriptive corpus.
Order by UTC date, job start, run ID, job ID; select the first attempted
scheduled job for a feature leg/date, even if invalid. Never replace an
invalid first attempt with a successful retry. Manual dispatches, other
attempts, absent CPU labels and incomplete logs remain descriptive evidence.
Multiple feature legs from one nightly are distinct strata, not extra
independent observations for the same cell.

CPU normalization collapses whitespace and removes only the literal
`(R)` / `(TM)` spelling marks; preserve the complete remaining model string.
Do not merge different EPYC/Xeon model numbers by family, vendor or prefix.
The four feature legs are the exact current nightly matrix strings, including
`default`; `crypto-bigint-scalar` is also required in every compiled harness.

**Code eligibility:** baseline crypto/harness sources are `b22718f`. The
pin-enforcement revision may change workflow/assurance tooling only. Before
C0, generate a research-only lockfile and build-input manifest covering SM2,
SM3, HMAC, curve/backend dependencies, harness, manifests, bench profile,
compiler full identity and target triple. Freeze their hashes in the study
record; never commit the ignored root lockfile. Archive that lockfile with
study evidence and use its exact resolution for the fresh calibration,
confirmation and control builds. Evidence capture must verify it before
compiling; drift makes the job ineligible and is reported rather than fixed
by regenerating the lockfile. Production dependency policy outside these
study builds remains unchanged.

The numerical protocol and structural controls are reviewed first. Build-only
preparation then freezes exact manifest/patch hashes before C0, without
examining new timing results. Relevant crypto/harness/build changes during
the study are incompatible observations, not a new baseline. For historical
logs, recover the actual environment-capture compiler and available
resolution metadata; missing fields stay unknown. An action tag or default-
toolchain message cannot stand in for the effective compiler.

**Runner images:** retain exact version and kernel as covariates. Pooling
within the fresh cohort across `ubuntu-24.04` image versions is permitted
only at the verified Rust 1.95.0/harness/build identity, to propose a
conservative envelope rather than estimate an image-invariant distribution.
Every included image subgroup must satisfy the resulting bound. Prospective
validation below separately checks each image actually observed; images never
validated never inherit a new bound. This limited pooling is not evidence
that image updates are inert.

## 3. Deterministic candidate table

Targets: `ct_fn_invert`, `ct_fp_invert`, `ct_sign_k_class`, `ct_hmac_sm3`.
Each existing nightly bound `B0` is 0.55. Retain fanout's 0.20 / EPYC 9V74
0.55 policy as unchanged rows; do not fit or tighten fanout in this protocol.
Do not change any other required target or the PR-smoke policy.

For a cell with n valid distinct-date observations sorted numerically as
`x[1] <= ... <= x[n]`, set `q90 = x[ceil(0.90*n)]` and
`B = max(0.20, 0.05 * ceil((2*q90)/0.05))`, using decimal arithmetic.
The factor 2 and grid 0.05 are fixed engineering margins, not estimated
confidence limits. Do not tune them after seeing derived candidates.

Apply these decisions in order:

1. If any included calibration job breaches the existing bound, record
   `investigation-required` and propose no override for that cell. Retain
   the breach; neither trimming nor lifting a bound resolves it.
2. Fewer than 20 distinct dates, or fewer than 5 in either chronological
   21-day half of the calibration interval: `insufficient-calibration`, no override.
3. If `B >= B0`, retain `B0` as `unchanged`; do not clamp an oversized fitted
   value downward and call it validated.
4. If any calibration observation exceeds B, mark `candidate-rejected`,
   no override. Do not increase B to absorb that outlier.
5. Otherwise freeze B as `proposed-tightening`. No candidate may raise B0.

Write every cell and fallback row, including exclusions and unchanged rows,
into the candidate artifact. Freeze the nonempty set A of all proposed
changes together with the corpus hash, rule version and exact environment
manifest in a reviewed commit before prospective observation starts.
If A is empty after the full calibration cohort, report `insufficient
evidence` for activating a new table; no confirmation window or control
dispatches are needed when there is no proposed change. Still publish the
completed calibration corpus and investigation findings. Do not manufacture a new
formula to ensure a nonempty set.

## 4. Prospective window and environment applicability

T0 is 00:00 UTC on the first date strictly after the candidate freeze commit
is accepted and the evaluator/control build manifests are ready. Record T0
and the exclusive endpoint T0+42 days before the first included scheduled
job. The window always spans all 42 dates; a failure may stop extra control
dispatches, but all subsequent scheduled evidence is still accounted for.

Keep the production workflows and gate verdicts unchanged. Apply the frozen
candidate offline to the scheduled logs under the calibration selection and
completeness rules. Count invalid/missing dates and excluded configurations;
no fresh threshold, tier downgrade, retry substitution or endpoint extension.
Every changed cell needs at least 20 valid distinct dates, every eligible
observation must pass, and every image-version/kernel subgroup seen in that
cell needs at least 3 valid distinct dates. A subgroup below that minimum is
insufficient evidence, not an excuse to drop the subgroup after inspection.

All candidate breaches reject the proposed table. Existing-gate breaches are
investigation items even outside A; no activation while one remains
unexplained in the evaluation corpus. Missing metadata and code/toolchain
changes are ineligible observations and may prevent required coverage.

Activation, if earned, is limited to the exact validated image-version/kernel
pairs and code/build identities listed with each changed cell. Missing or
new environment identities fall back to current policy. This can make
activation short-lived as runner images roll; a new image needs a new frozen
validation, not automatic inheritance. Report the resulting coverage cost.

## 5. Isolated leak controls

Control builds start from the same frozen base and differ only by the
predeclared injection for one target at a time. Never commit leaky crate
sources, publish patched crates or change the production nightly. A
manual-only research workflow may create temporary patched workspaces,
run the original bench and export evidence; its runner label/toolchain stay
identical to the nightly. Its own 90-minute budget does not change the
nightly's pinned timeout. Build identities and patch SHA-256s are frozen
before any control timing is measured; source/compile validation may not
select or retune an effect based on timing results.

The modest effect is **16 dependent field squarings** for invert/sign and
**one extra SM3 compression block** for HMAC. The gross effect is **1024
squarings** or **64 extra compression blocks** respectively. These operation
counts define the minimum effects this study attempts to detect; they do
not assert a portable nanosecond or tau effect. Effects smaller or different
from these remain unvalidated even when a cell passes.

| Target | Injection site and predicate | Work, with result consumed by `core::hint::black_box` |
|---|---|---|
| `ct_fn_invert` | Inside the existing timed closure, before `x.invert()`; derive predicate from the highest input bit of `x.retrieve()` | Start with x; execute 16 or 1024 dependent scalar-field squarings |
| `ct_fp_invert` | Inside the existing timed closure, before `x.invert()`; same highest-bit predicate | Start with x; execute 16 or 1024 dependent base-field squarings |
| `ct_sign_k_class` | Isolated `try_sign_once`, immediately after nonce sampling; highest bit of sampled nonce | Start with k; execute 16 or 1024 dependent scalar-field squarings per fixed retry |
| `ct_hmac_sm3` | Isolated one-shot HMAC, before inner hashing; highest bit of `key[0]` for the fixed 32-byte test keys | Separate scratch Sm3 state; absorb 1 or 64 identical 64-byte blocks formed by repeating the key twice, consume state without finalizing or changing the original HMAC result |

Before timing, validate each optimized control binary, not just its source:
verify the retained input predicate, direction, dependency chain and exact
work count by optimized IR/disassembly plus an untimed instrumented operation-
count check on the same source recipe. For field work put an optimizer barrier
on the initial state and both sides of every square:
`state = black_box(black_box(state).square())`. This prevents constant folding
even for the signing nonce 1; inspect the emitted work to confirm it. For
HMAC, apply equivalent barriers to the scratch state and block input and
verify the number of compression calls/rounds retained. The sham must retain
the same predicate evaluation and setup, consuming its predicate through a
barrier even when it adds no work. A missing branch effect, eliminated chain,
or mismatched sham is an invalid build; fix structural implementation and
freeze a new build identity before measurement, without selecting by timing.

Each input pair must be source-checked to take opposite predicate values;
assert this outside timing during control validation. Keep original crypto
outputs and class inputs unchanged. All secret-derived branching in this
matrix is confined to intentionally leaky research builds. Invert predicates
add retrieval overhead; a matched sham build executes the same predicate and
setup with zero extra operations. Compare sham against unmodified baseline
to prevent attributing setup effects to the intended injection.

For each target create five variants: sham, modest-on-left, modest-on-right,
gross-on-left, gross-on-right, plus the unmodified baseline. Both directions
are required because existing signed effects may cancel an injected effect
on one class. Source predicates choose real input values, not the harness's
public class label. Do not amplify or change the predicate after failure.

Each research job first runs the unmodified full five-pass nightly harness
and records its ordinary verdict. Then run five passes of each target's
variants, filtering the original bench by exact target name and running
`negative_control` in every pass. The target function, timed window, input
classes, sample count, aggregation and bound remain the same as production;
filtering and build setup are recorded differences. The matched unmodified
filtered baseline must pass alongside the full-run baseline; otherwise the
job cannot validate that cell's operating context.

Variant order rotates by pass index over the six variants and target order
rotates by dispatch index over the four targets, both modulo list length;
write the actual order to the manifest before starting the job. Build all
variants before timing to keep compilation outside the experiment sequence.
Retain RNG seeds, signed outputs and wall times. Existing raw `--out` export
is not used as class-labelled evidence without verifying its format.

Classify control evidence **per changed cell**, separating measurement
usability from the observed result. Apply these rules in order:

1. A measurement (full-run baseline, filtered baseline, sham or one leaky
   variant) is **usable** only with the verified build/control identity,
   required CPU/image metadata, complete finite five-pass output at the
   declared budget and negative-control magnitude >1.0 in every pass.
   A broken build, absent metadata, incomplete output or failed liveness
   makes that measurement unusable; its tau values establish neither a
   passing control nor a rejection. A timeout does not erase another
   measurement already completed with all these requirements satisfied.
2. **Any usable baseline or sham median above B rejects the table** as a
   failure of the proposed operating context, even if another measurement
   is missing or unusable. It is not merely invalid evidence and cannot be
   rerun away. Equality passes for baseline and sham.
3. Once both baselines and the sham are usable and pass B, **any one usable
   leaky variant median at or below B rejects the table**. One escape in
   one job is sufficient; detections in other variants or jobs cannot
   override it. A missing different variant does not conceal this escape.
   If a baseline or sham is unusable, an otherwise measured leaky variant
   cannot validate or reject sensitivity for that job's operating context.
4. A job counts toward a cell's required three distinct control dates only
   when both baselines and sham are usable and pass B, and **all four**
   leaky variants are usable and each has median > B. Otherwise it adds no
   control-coverage date; absent a rejection above, retain the missing or
   unusable evidence for the deadline's coverage decision.

Rejection takes precedence over missing coverage across the entire frozen
set A. The following cases pin the interpretation before implementation:

| Evidence for a changed cell | Outcome contribution |
|---|---|
| Both baselines and sham usable and <= B; all four leaky variants usable and > B | One control-coverage date, subject to distinct-date counting |
| Same passing context; one usable leaky variant equals B or falls below B | Reject, even if the other three variants or other jobs detect |
| Same passing context; one leaky variant escapes and another is missing | Reject; the usable escape is retained |
| A usable baseline or sham exceeds B; another measurement is missing | Reject operating context |
| A baseline or sham is unusable; no usable baseline/sham exceeds B | No coverage date and no sensitivity conclusion from that job |
| Context passes; one leaky variant is missing/unusable and the other usable variants exceed B | No coverage date; insufficient evidence at the deadline if coverage remains short |

An existing production-gate failure still requires investigation under the
ordinary-run rules, independently of these control classifications.

Dispatch at most two four-leg runs per seven-day block of the 42-day window,
one on offsets 1 and 4 from the block's start, without targeting a CPU by
rerunning until it appears. Three valid control jobs on distinct dates per
changed cell are required, with at least one on every prospectively observed
image/kernel subgroup. The same job can supply independent *target results*,
but its five passes do not count as independent jobs. Missing rare-CPU or
image coverage at the deadline is insufficient evidence. No late dispatches
or increased budget after inspecting outcomes.

## 6. September 17 investigation

Preserve run `35185121870`, failed job `105085426488`, on EPYC 9V74,
image `20260907.300.1`. Inspect source/build identities, parser arithmetic,
per-pass signed outputs, crop counts, same-job twin/control values and
same-run feature/CPU differences. Those comparisons are diagnostic; they
do not establish that a platform-dependent difference is harmless.

The [archived-log recheck](v1.15-sentinel-investigation.md) found reported crop counts of
0.097–0.099M. The median absolute reported tau is 0.61833; using the declared
100000 budget instead gives about 0.61462. Both exceed 0.55, so this event
is not explained by the tiny-crop failure seen in the F21 local study.
Record this narrow finding with the original five outputs in the evidence.
The same log exposes the effective Rust 1.98.1/toolchain-selection mismatch;
it is a confound requiring re-baselining, not proof that the compiler caused
the breach. Do not claim a physical mechanism without additional evidence.

If no mechanism is established, the final report says `cause unresolved`;
the 9V74 fp-invert cell remains investigation-required at its current
sentinel. No repaired parser or alternate observable is silently substituted
into this protocol. A newly established crypto defect follows the project's
security-disclosure process and blocks activation until addressed separately.

## 7. Outcomes and deliverables

Reject takes precedence over insufficient evidence. For set A as frozen:

- **Rejected:** any prospective candidate-bound breach, any usable
  baseline/sham median > B, or any one usable leaky variant median <= B
  when both baselines and sham are usable and pass B, as defined in §5.
  Keep current gates; report all rows and the first rejection. Unusable
  evidence cannot by itself reject, or erase another usable rejection.
- **Insufficient evidence:** no rejection, but A is empty, any coverage or
  build/environment requirement is missing, or a relevant existing-gate
  breach remains unexplained at the deadline. Keep current gates.
- **Eligible for activation review:** nonempty A, all row coverage and
  control requirements pass, zero prospective candidate failures and no
  unresolved relevant existing-gate breach. Submit one activation PR for
  all of A, limited to validated environments. Do not cherry-pick a subset.

Preserve both corpus CSV/manifests, deterministic derivation and evaluation
code with meaningful parser/decision tests, candidate freeze, prospective
observations, controls and build hashes, investigation note and row-level
report. Record exactly one table outcome even when activation is deferred.
A failed or sparse study is a valid result; missing implementation or
unperformed required evidence work is not completion.

If activation is eligible, separately update nightly policy, reviewed
fingerprints and `SECURITY.md` claims to the demonstrated sensitivity only.
Validate boundary cases, duplicate/missing passes, unknown/incompatible
fallback, liveness and feature selection; pass the policy mutation suite,
PR smoke and a dispatched nightly. Retain version 1.13.0 and publish nothing. Expected elapsed measurement time
is 42 days of calibration plus 42 days of confirmation, with a build/freeze
boundary between them; do not hide that calendar cost behind “42 days”.
