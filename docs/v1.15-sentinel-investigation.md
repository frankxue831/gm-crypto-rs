default, then explicitly notes a `stable` override from the repository's


Both dudect workflows invoke bare `cargo bench`; their action tag alone does
not pin the effective compiler in this checkout. The previous scope's
fixed-toolchain assumption is therefore unsupported for this job. Record
the compiler from the environment-capture step when stratifying historical
evidence, not the setup action's explicit version check. This mismatch does
not prove that compiler drift caused the sentinel breach.

## What this establishes

The event is **not explained by a tiny winning percentile crop**: all five
reported counts are near the 100000-sample budget, unlike the few-sample
crop in the separate F21 local study. Normalizing by the declared budget
only changes this job's median slightly and does not remove the breach.
All five signed t values have the same sign. Liveness fired and the twin
remained quiet while the fp-invert target breached its sentinel.

The job log does not retain labelled per-sample timings sufficient to
reconstruct every percentile test. It does not establish whether the class
separation is caused by compiler-generated code, operand-dependent hardware
behavior, scheduling, or another confound. Sign consistency and a quiet
other-CPU result cannot resolve that causal question.

**Disposition:** retain the observation as an unexplained sentinel breach
on unchanged source. Preserve the current policy; do not remove the
sentinel, raise its bound, or call the event a false positive on this
recheck alone. The numerical protocol must account for this unresolved cell
and use isolated controls to assess any new sensitivity claim.
